# Marleigh Math Genius Discovery Pilot — 2026-11-03

## Purpose and status

This document preserves the first live, voluntary child self-reflection pilot for the Garvey Genius Map. It is a product-design and learning record, not a clinical assessment, diagnosis, academic placement decision, or fixed description of the learner.

The pilot's purpose was to discover how a child describes her own mathematical thinking, motivation, learning preferences, and invention goals. It complements, but does not replace, the existing parent observation assessment. The learner chose to participate and was free to answer in her own words rather than accept forced-choice wording.

**Learner:** Marleigh (first name only)

**Format tested:** 14 conversational math-and-invention reflection prompts, initially introduced with some A/B/C/D choices and then adapted to open response when those choices did not capture the learner's thinking.

## What the pilot established

1. A child self-reflection must not force one “genius type.” Several strengths can be active in one moment.
2. “Explain it in my own words” is a required response path, not an edge case.
3. The child should be able to select multiple resonant choices before adding their own explanation.
4. Scenario-based prompts about real mathematical work, projects, inventions, and challenge are more revealing than generic trait statements.
5. Results should report **strengths the learner recognizes in themselves today**, accompanied by evidence from their own words. They must not describe a permanent label or a deficit.
6. The assessment should remain optional, untimed, skippable, and enjoyable. Voluntary engagement itself can provide useful qualitative evidence of curiosity and ownership, but it must not be scored as a trait.

## Complete pilot transcript and evidence log

### 1. Starting a confusing problem

**Prompt:** When a math problem looks confusing at first, what do you usually do?

**Marleigh's response:** She said she takes the pieces she understands and works with those first so she can somehow understand the rest.

**Captured strategy:** Break a hard problem into understandable parts; build from known information toward unknown information.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **RS — Thinking & Problem Solving:** decomposition and structured entry point.
- **SR — Focus & Self-Control:** selects a workable first step rather than becoming stuck in the whole problem.

### 2. After a hard idea clicks

**Prompt:** When you understand a hard math idea, what do you feel like doing next?

**Initial choices:** move on; try another similar problem; test whether the idea works in a different type of problem; explain it to someone else.

**Marleigh's response:** She chose the practice-and-transfer direction: she works other related problems to “lock it in,” understand the concept more, and see what it looks like in different problems.

**Captured strategy:** Use similar-but-different problems to consolidate a concept and test transfer.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **CQ — Curiosity & Love of Learning:** seeks deeper conceptual understanding.
- **RS — Thinking & Problem Solving:** tests transfer across related examples.
- **PS — Effort & Resilience:** voluntarily continues practice after initial success.

### 3. Comparing methods

**Prompt:** If you and someone else get the same math answer using different methods, what do you think?

**Marleigh's response:** She said she would notice the other method, wonder whether it works for every problem, and wonder whether it is more efficient than her method.

**Captured strategy:** Compare methods, test generalizability, and consider efficiency.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **RS:** compares the conditions under which a method works.
- **CQ:** asks questions beyond the immediate answer.
- **FB — Learning From Feedback:** treats another person's method as useful information for improving her own work.

### 4. Finding an error

**Prompt:** When a teacher or hint shows that one step was off, what do you do first?

**Marleigh's response:** She looks back to find where the problem went wrong, notices a missed number or step, laughs it off, and carries on.

**Captured strategy:** Trace an error to its source, correct it without over-identifying with the mistake, and continue.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **FB:** uses correction to revise work.
- **RS:** traces the causal point of the error.
- **PS:** recovers quickly and continues after a mistake.

### 5. What feels exciting in math

**Prompt:** Which math moment is most exciting: finishing a hard problem, seeing a pattern, finding a clearer method, or using math to invent something?

**Marleigh's response:** She connected with all of them. She enjoys the uncertainty of a hard problem becoming correct, seeing patterns, finding clearer methods, and using math to invent and express creativity.

**Captured strategy:** Enjoy challenge, pattern recognition, clearer methods, and creative application together.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **PS:** enjoys the effort-to-success arc.
- **RS:** notices patterns.
- **CR — Creativity & Idea Thinking:** values new and clearer approaches.
- **DE — Interests & Passion Areas:** connects math to invention and creative expression.

### 6. When no listed response fits

**Prompt:** When there is no immediate answer, choose any moves that sound like you: write what you know; draw or make an example; try a simpler version; look for a pattern; ask for a small hint and continue.

**Marleigh's response:** She said none of the options really captured her approach. When a new concept is not clicking, she asks for a deeper explanation with all the steps because she needs to understand the exact steps to take to reach the answer.

**Captured strategy:** Identify when foundational instruction is missing; ask for an explanation that makes the process intelligible rather than merely requesting an answer.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **CQ:** seeks the “why” and “how.”
- **FB:** asks for actionable instructional feedback.
- **SR:** recognizes what information is needed to continue productively.

**Assessment design finding:** A required own-words path is essential. This answer would have been lost with a forced-choice-only instrument.

### 7. Using a solved example

**Prompt:** Once the steps click, what do you do with the next related problem?

**Marleigh's response:** She keeps the completed problem as an example to help with the next problem that is like it.

**Captured strategy:** Use a worked example as a reference and bridge to related work.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **RS:** compare a new problem with an understood structure.
- **SR:** uses an organized reference strategy.
- **FB:** carries instruction forward into independent work.

### 8. Staying interested during sustained math work

**Prompt:** What helps you stay focused and not get mentally tired or bored while doing a lot of math?

**Marleigh's response:** She pulls up different kinds of related problems instead of repeating the same type. Once she gets the hang of them, she moves to harder questions so she is both avoiding boredom and challenging herself.

**Captured strategy:** Vary related practice and self-increase difficulty when ready.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **SR:** manages engagement intentionally.
- **CQ:** wants varied forms of an idea.
- **PS:** chooses increased challenge.
- **DE:** demonstrates self-directed interest in continued growth.

### 9. Designing a perfect math lesson

**Prompt:** If you could design your perfect math lesson, what would it include?

**Marleigh's response:** She would use a Canva slideshow so she could add her own details and make the lesson visually interesting rather than working from a paper full of questions. She would vary questions and increase the challenge as she starts getting it.

**Captured strategy:** Design a visual, personally engaging, varied, progressively challenging learning environment.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **CR:** wants to author the learning experience.
- **DE:** visual design supports sustained engagement.
- **SR:** recognizes conditions that support attention.
- **PS:** welcomes progressive challenge.

### 10. What success means

**Prompt:** When you finish a hard problem and get it right, what feels best: the correct answer, understanding why it works, proving you could do it, or something else?

**Marleigh's response:** All three matter. She feels proud for not giving up, values understanding how the problem works, and is happy to prove to herself that she can solve a new hard question.

**Captured strategy:** Define success as persistence + independent solution + conceptual understanding.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **PS:** values pushing through a difficult task.
- **RS:** values logic and explanation, not answer-only completion.
- **CQ:** prioritizes understanding.
- **DE:** gains intrinsic satisfaction from growth.

### 11. Future application: Home Assistance robot

**Prompt:** If math could help you build anything in the future, what would you use it for?

**Marleigh's response:** She described her H.A. (Home Assistance) robot idea for seniors who need help with daily tasks. For example, if something falls, the robot could extend an arm, pick it up, and return it to the person so they can remain independent if they wish.

**Captured strategy:** Connect math and engineering to a real human need; imagine functional system behavior and a design purpose.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **CR:** produces an original applied invention concept.
- **RS:** converts a real-world need into solvable robot functions.
- **DE:** strong engagement with robotics, engineering, and helpful technology.
- **CQ:** seeks the knowledge needed to make the idea real.

### 12. Finding prerequisites for an invention

**Prompt:** If you have the H.A. robot idea but do not yet know the math or science required for one part, what would you do next?

**Marleigh's response:** She would ask which math concepts are required, study and work up to those concepts, then use them to solve problems such as arm length and how the robot recognizes speech.

**Captured strategy:** Scope a project’s prerequisite knowledge; study foundations; apply concepts to design constraints.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **CQ:** identifies the information needed.
- **RS:** connects concepts to applied constraints.
- **PS:** takes a long-path approach toward a complex goal.
- **DE:** sustained robotics/engineering motivation.

### 13. Working through prerequisite steps

**Prompt:** When a project has many steps before the fun part, what keeps you going?

**Marleigh's response:** The motivation of reaching the fun part keeps her going. She understands that X, Y, and Z have to happen first, and completing them is the path to the rewarding work she is excited about.

**Captured strategy:** Use a valued future goal to sustain effort through prerequisites.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **SR:** organizes present actions around a known sequence.
- **PS:** maintains effort through less-preferred steps.
- **DE:** uses meaningful interest as intrinsic motivation.

### 14. Desired mathematical growth

**Prompt:** What math or problem-solving ability would you like to strengthen, and why?

**Marleigh's response:** She wants to come up with her own methods because different methods fit different problems. She wants different methods so each problem becomes clearer and clearer.

**Captured strategy:** Develop a flexible method toolkit; choose or invent approaches based on problem structure.

**Genius Map evidence:**

- **CR:** wants to generate methods.
- **RS:** understands that methods have different conditions of usefulness.
- **CQ:** seeks clarity and deeper understanding across problem types.

## Pilot result: current Math Genius profile

This is a current self-reflection profile based on one voluntary conversation. It is not a ranking, diagnosis, grade, or prediction.

### Strongest current signals

1. **Thinking & Problem Solving (RS):** breaks work into understandable parts; uses worked examples; compares methods; checks transfer, patterns, and efficiency.
2. **Curiosity & Love of Learning (CQ):** asks for deeper step-by-step explanation; values why/how; wants to test whether an idea generalizes.
3. **Creativity & Idea Thinking (CR):** wants to design visual lessons, create methods, and apply mathematics to inventions.

### Strong supporting signals

- **Effort & Resilience (PS):** chooses harder work, recovers from errors, and sees prerequisite work as a path to a goal.
- **Learning From Feedback (FB):** traces errors, requests the needed explanation, and uses worked examples as transfer tools.
- **Focus & Self-Control (SR):** manages engagement through variation, challenge progression, and sequenced goals.
- **Interests & Passion Areas (DE):** strong robotics, engineering, visual design, invention, and helpful-technology interest.

### Learner-centered learning statement

> Help me understand the idea deeply; then let me test it, compare it, vary it, and use it to build something real.

### Current growth direction

> Build a flexible toolkit of methods, understand when each method fits, and create new approaches when a problem calls for one.

## Child assessment v1 product requirements

### Experience principles

- Title: **Discover Your Math Genius** or **How Your Math Mind Works**.
- Voluntary entry; no forced completion; no timer; every question has **Skip for now**.
- Use 10–14 short scenario cards. Show progress only as encouragement, not pressure.
- Every card offers **Choose any that sound like you** and **Tell it your way**.
- Include read-aloud support and a low-reading-load version.
- Do not show trait names while the child answers. Show a warm, evidence-backed reflection at the end.
- Never convert a low or skipped response into a weakness label.

### Result rules

- Report a **combination** of active strengths, not a single type.
- Show the child’s own words next to each strength as the evidence.
- Use “showing up today,” “you described,” and “you may enjoy” language.
- Pair each strength with one chosen next adventure, not a remedial prescription.
- Keep parent observation, child self-reflection, academic performance, and game activity as distinct evidence streams.

### Essential response model

```json
{
  "question_id": "child_math_v1_06",
  "selected_options": ["ask_for_deeper_explanation"],
  "own_words": "I need all the steps because it might be new and I do not know the exact steps yet.",
  "skipped": false,
  "response_mode": "own_words",
  "trait_tags": ["CQ", "FB", "SR"]
}
```

The system must preserve `own_words` as first-class evidence. It must not flatten the response into a single numeric value or discard it after a trait summary is generated.

## Acceptance criteria from this pilot

- A learner can finish with only open responses, only selected options, or a blend.
- A learner can choose more than one response on a card.
- A learner can skip without a penalty or deficit label.
- The result can accurately represent Marleigh’s statement that she learns through deep explanation, worked examples, related variation, increasing challenge, and real invention goals.
- The result shows at least three active strengths and supporting strengths without claiming the learner has one permanent “type.”
- The H.A. robot response remains visible as an example of interests and purpose-driven application, subject to the child profile’s privacy controls.
